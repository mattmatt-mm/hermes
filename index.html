Lesson 6 · tech

Concurrency basics: threads, locks, and lock-free ideas

  • Threads vs processes — A process owns memory; threads share it — which is why threads are fast to coordinate and easy to break.
  • Race conditions and data races — When two threads touch the same data without ordering, results become nondeterministic — the core bug class of concurrent code.
  • Mutexes and locks — The standard fix: guard shared state with a lock, and understand the cost — contention, deadlock, and priority inversion.
  • Atomics and memory ordering — Hardware-level operations that update a value indivisibly, letting you skip locks for simple counters and flags.
  • Lock-free thinking — Why low-latency trading systems avoid locks entirely: compare-and-swap loops, single-writer queues, and the price you pay in complexity.
